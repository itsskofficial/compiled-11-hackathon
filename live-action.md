# Warden — the live-action shots

Everything we physically film with people in it. Screen captures (Shots 2, 4, 7) are not here — see `script.md` for the full cut.

**Five shots have humans. Only two have anyone speaking on camera.** That's the whole performance burden: two short pieces to lens. Everything else is body language over voiceover recorded later.

| Shot | Who's in it | Speaking on camera? | Setup |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Cold open | Presenter | **Yes** — sync sound | B (neutral wall) |
| 3 The intruder | Intruder | No | A (the door) |
| 5 Voice-down | Intruder | No — product audio only | A (the door) |
| 6 The call | Presenter | No — product audio, VO added later | C (manager's location) |
| 8 Close | Presenter | **Yes** — sync sound | B (neutral wall) |

---

## The three setups

Build each once, shoot everything in it, then strike. Do not revisit a setup.

### Setup A — the door *(Shots 3, 5)*

This is "Maple Grove, Unit 4B." Any real door with a working handle, or a doorway with a visible frame.

- **Laptop webcam** on a table or box stack, **chest height**, 3–4 m back, angled slightly down. Door centred with floor and wall around it. This framing ends up in the evidence PDF, so make it look like a security camera, not a laptop on a desk.
- **Phone A** on a tripod or taped to a chair, beside the webcam, **wide** — full body floor to above head.
- **Lighting:** overhead on. Never shoot toward a window or lamp — backlight silhouettes the intruder and degrades the classification as well as the picture. If dim, put a desk lamp behind Phone A aimed at the door.
- **Lock exposure and focus** on Phone A before rolling, or it re-exposes when someone enters and the shot pulses.

**Shots 3 and 5 are one continuous performance** — the intruder approaches, tries the handle, the voice fires, they leave. Shoot it as one take and cut it in two in the edit. Do not reset between them.

### Setup B — neutral wall *(Shots 1, 8)*

- Plain wall. No whiteboard with someone else's notes, no venue signage, no clutter.
- **Phone A static, landscape, medium shot — chest up.** Lens at the presenter's **eye height**. Below eye height reads as amateur instantly.
- Presenter looks **straight down the lens**, never at the operator.
- Shoot 1 and 8 back to back so framing matches exactly. The film bookends.

### Setup C — the manager *(Shot 6)*

**This must not look like Setup A.** Narratively the on-call manager is somewhere else entirely — that's the point of the call. If the intruder and the manager appear to be in the same room, the story breaks.

Different wall, different angle, ideally a different room. Medium shot, waist up, so the phone coming out of the pocket is visible.

## Cast and wardrobe

- **PRESENTER** — Shots 1, 6, 8. **Same clothing in all three.** Solid mid-tone colour, no busy patterns (they moiré on phone cameras), no logos, no lanyard.
- **INTRUDER** — Shots 3, 5. **Ordinary clothes.** A jacket and jeans. Whatever a person actually wears.
- **OPERATOR** — never on camera. Runs the laptop, starts and stops recordings, calls action.

> **The single most important wardrobe note: no hoodie, no mask, no gloves.** The instinct is to dress the intruder as a burglar and it is wrong twice over. It reads as costume and kills credibility — and a person in ordinary clothes calmly testing a door is genuinely more unsettling than someone in a balaclava. It also matches the classification we want: *"no uniform"* is the model noting an absence of authorization, not a costume.

## SHOT 1 — Cold open *(Setup B · presenter · sync sound)*

**Framing:** medium, chest up, eye-height lens, plain wall.

**Words, exactly:**

> "A twenty-four-seven security guard post costs about two hundred thousand dollars a year.
>
> Here's what replaces it."

**Direction:**
- **Do not smile before the first word.** No windup, no "hi, we're team Warden." The first frame should feel like a product, not an introduction.
- Pause noticeably between the two sentences — longer than feels natural. It reads as confidence on camera and gives the editor a cut point.
- Land "it" and **hold still for two full seconds** before the operator stops recording. Editors need handles; a shot that cuts on the last syllable is unusable.
- Deliver it flat and certain. This is a statement of fact about someone's budget, not a pitch.

**Takes:** minimum 3. Don't review between them — shoot three, then pick.

## SHOT 3 — The intruder *(Setup A · intruder · no dialogue)*

**Framing:** wide, full body, door in frame.

**Blocking, beat by beat:**

| Beat | Action |
|---|---|
| 0–2s | Enter frame at a **normal walking pace** |
| 2–3s | One brief glance over the shoulder. Once, not twice |
| 3–5s | Approach the door, stop square to it |
| 5–7s | Try the handle — **gently first** |
| 7–10s | Try it again, **harder**, with a shoulder lean |

**Direction:**
- **Never look at the camera.** Not once, not at the end of the take.
- **Do not creep.** The instinct is to sneak, and it reads as pantomime. Walk like you're allowed to be there — that's what real intrusion looks like on real footage.
- **The second handle attempt is the shot.** One try reads as "a person is present." Two, with force, is what makes the model say *attempting*. That word is the entire product claim, so give it something to see.
- Stay at the door at the end. Shot 5 continues from here.

**Takes:** 3. Vary the handle-force slightly between takes so there's a choice in the edit.

## SHOT 5 — Voice-down *(Setup A · intruder · product audio)*

**Continues from Shot 3 without resetting.**

**Product audio** — Miso One, played from the file. Nobody speaks this:

> "This property is monitored. Security has been notified. Leave the premises now."

**Blocking:**

| Beat | Action |
|---|---|
| On the **first word** | Freeze mid-reach. Not after the sentence — on "This" |
| +1s | Hold completely still. One full second of nothing |
| +2s | Glance toward the speaker, briefly |
| +3s | Release the handle, step back |
| +4s | Leave frame **at a walk** |

**Direction:**
- **The freeze must land on the first word.** Freezing after the sentence finishes is the single most common way this beat dies.
- **Walk out, don't run.** Someone leaving calmly is more believable and more chilling than someone fleeing. It also matches the disposition we're claiming — *deterred by voice*, not *pursued*.
- Don't stare at the speaker. A glance, then go.

**Audio check before rolling:** laptop speakers must be loud enough for Phone A's mic to hear cleanly from where it's standing. Test with the intruder out of position first.

**Takes:** minimum 3, and expect to need them. This is the most satisfying beat in the film and the easiest to over-play.

## SHOT 6 — The call *(Setup C · presenter · product audio)*

**Framing:** medium, waist up, so the phone leaving the pocket is visible.

**Blocking:**

| Beat | Action |
|---|---|
| 0–2s | Standing, phone in pocket. **Wait for the real ring** |
| 2–4s | Take it out, look at the screen |
| 4–6s | Answer, then **tap speaker** so the room hears it |
| 6–18s | The Phonely audio plays (below). Presenter listens |
| 18–21s | Press **3** — thumb movement clearly visible |
| 21–23s | Lower the phone. Hold |

**Product audio** — Phonely, the actual call:

> "Warden security operations, priority alert. Verified intruder at Maple Grove, Unit four-B — vacant unit, day twelve of turnover. One adult at the rear entry, no uniform, attempting the door. Voice deterrent has fired.
>
> Press one to dispatch a guard. Press two to notify police. Press three to dismiss."

**Direction — the key note:**
- **Play it as routine, not dramatic.** No alarm, no widened eyes. This manager gets these calls; they're competent and slightly bored. Routine competence is far more convincing than urgency, and it's the thing that sells *service* rather than *gadget* — the system is working normally, and this is what normal looks like.
- **Wait for the actual ring.** Don't mime reaching for a phone that hasn't rung; the timing is always visibly off.
- Make the thumb press readable. Don't hide the screen with your hand.

**Phone hygiene before this take:** Do Not Disturb **off** (it has to ring), every other app's notifications silenced, ringer at max, auto-lock **Never**, and **wipe the screen** — smears are very visible on camera.

**Takes:** 3, but each needs a fresh trigger from the operator. Coordinate.

## SHOT 8 — Close *(Setup B · presenter · sync sound)*

**Framing: match Shot 1 exactly.** Same position, same wall, same lens height. Shoot immediately after Shot 1 while nothing has moved.

**Words, exactly:**

> "Everyone else sells you cameras.
>
> Competitors see pixels. Because we deliver the service, we see outcomes — and outcomes are what train the model, set the margin, and price the insurance.
>
> That was a two-hundred-thousand-dollar-a-year guard post, replaced.
>
> Now imagine forty thousand doors."

**Direction:**
- The middle sentence is the densest thing in the film. **Slow down through it** and put a real beat before "and outcomes are what train the model."
- "Now imagine forty thousand doors" — say it and **stop**. No thank-you, no team name, no wave. Hold the frame two seconds.
- Same flat certainty as Shot 1. The register is institutional throughout.

**Takes:** minimum 4. This one carries the pitch and it's worth the extra two minutes.

## Shooting order

Not the edit order. Protect continuity, do the hard things while there's still time.

1. **Setup A — Shots 3 and 5 together**, three or more takes. Hardest to redo, most dependent on the product running. Do these first.
2. **Setup C — Shot 6.** Needs the phone path working and the operator triggering.
3. **Setup B — Shots 1 and 8 back to back.** Easiest, fully independent of the product. These can be shot even if the build is still broken.
4. **Voiceover** last, somewhere quiet, once picture is locked.

**If the build is behind:** shoot Setup B now. Shots 1 and 8 need nothing but a wall and a phone, they're 40 seconds of the film, and having them in the can removes pressure from everything else.

---

## What a failed take looks like

Call it and go again — don't try to save it in the edit.

- Intruder looks at the camera
- The freeze lands after the voice line instead of on its first word
- Intruder runs out of frame
- Presenter looks at the operator instead of the lens
- Presenter smiles before the first line of Shot 1
- The take cuts on the final syllable with no handle
- Phone A re-exposed mid-shot (the picture visibly pulses)
- Anyone's shadow crosses the door frame from behind the camera
